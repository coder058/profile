Energy Monitor

Can two electricity prices be compared fairly—and what was generating power at the same time? Energy Monitor lines up matching delivery periods and keeps missing or delayed readings visible, so a comparison can be checked instead of guessed.

PUBLIC EUROPEAN ENERGY DATA · READ-ONLY

Open the Energy Monitor

The question this solves

A price from one hour cannot be fairly compared with generation reported for another hour. Different providers also use different time intervals, units and update schedules. This monitor puts comparable records side by side and shows the gaps; it does not claim that generation caused a price move or predict what comes next.

What a visitor can check
QuestionWhat the monitor showsWhy it helps
What did two markets charge for the same period?Prices lined up by delivery time and interval length.Different hours are not presented as a valid comparison.

Is a reading missing or late?Gaps, source links and the time the monitor received the data.People can judge the evidence instead of seeing a made-up zero or a hidden stale value.

Try it in three steps

  1. Open the monitor and choose the Netherlands and a completed day.
  2. Add another available market to compare prices for matching delivery periods.
  3. Select a time on the chart to inspect the readings and open their source links. Some countries have generation data but no connected price feed.

How the pieces fit together

The monitor reads public data when a visitor selects a view, checks that each response has the expected units and time periods, then displays the records without filling gaps. Its local research database is separate from the public dashboard.

What it does not claim

The monitor is a read-only research tool. It does not produce a validated forecast, measure exchange-to-screen latency, identify a cause for a price move, or submit trades. The hosted dashboard has a short-lived response cache, not a permanent archive of what each source published at every earlier point in time.
