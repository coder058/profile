“WTI” is not spot

06 September 2026AmsterdamJordi Lluis

I am going to write about energy in this journal. The first honest sentence is about the file I actually have.

Pattern Forge ships a recorded market labelled WTI Oil. The catalog is explicit: it is a Polymarket Perps hourly series, not a CME futures settlement and not a cash WTI price. Missing hours are not filled. The last captured bar was omitted because it may still have been forming when the recording stopped.

I read those numbers from the committed JSON on 6 September 2026. I did not rebuild the parquet. I did not splice a live oil feed on top.

How Pattern Forge is allowed to use it

Replay in that workspace only supplies candles up to the cursor. Later prices stay out of the indicator. Incomplete higher-timeframe groups are dropped rather than guessed. That is the point of the tool, and it is the method this journal will keep when a chart is attached to a sentence.

The public demo does not serve PostgreSQL. Locally and in CI, closed candles can be stored. That database is not this recording’s venue, and it is not a warehouse of official energy statistics.

Conclusion I will defend: an energy view that starts from this file is a view of a perpetual-contract recording with holes. It is not a view of physical barrels, OPEC, or a settled WTI future. If I write a later post that forgets that, the post is wrong.

What I will use the project for next: walk the same recording under replay, mark the 42 missing hours, and only then ask whether a move is even visible on complete bars. No direction, no target, no “oil is going to.”
